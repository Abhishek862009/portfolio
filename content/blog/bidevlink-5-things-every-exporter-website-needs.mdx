A buyer finds your name on a trade list or in a directory. The next thing they usually do is look for your website. They want to know three things: what do you sell, are you a real business, and how can they contact you.

Your website does not need to be big. It needs to answer those three questions clearly. Here is a simple checklist. Check your own website against it.

## 1. Product pages with clear details and photos

A buyer cannot ask you a question before they have even decided to write to you. So your product page should answer the basic ones in advance.

- The product name and a short, plain description
- Key specifications, such as size, grade, material or packaging options
- Clear photos

If you export more than one product, give each product its own section or page. A buyer who is looking for one product should not have to search through everything else.

## 2. Contact details on the first screen

Many websites hide the contact details on a separate page, deep in the menu. Put your WhatsApp number, email and phone where a visitor sees them without scrolling or clicking.

Add one clear button, such as "Send enquiry" or "WhatsApp us". Make the next step easy.

## 3. Company details that a buyer can verify

Buyers work with people they have never met, so they look for proof that the business is real.

- Your full company name, address and city
- The registrations and certificates you hold, such as your IEC, FSSAI or other approvals that apply to your product
- A short "About" section: who you are, what you make or export and from where

Only list what you really have. A wrong or exaggerated claim does more harm than leaving it out.

## 4. Real photos of your own products

Stock photos are easy to spot, and buyers have seen them many times. A photo of your own product, your own packing or your own workplace tells the buyer that this is a real business.

You do not need a professional studio. Clear, well-lit photos from a good phone are a good start. Show the product from a few angles and show the packing.

## 5. A site that works well on a phone

Many people open links on their phone first, including a link you send on WhatsApp or email. If the text is tiny, the buttons are hard to tap or the page loads slowly, the visitor may leave.

Open your website on your own phone and try it. Can you read it, find the product and tap the contact button easily?

## Keep it simple and keep it current

A website with five clear sections is better than a big site that is out of date. Remove old product information and update your contact details when they change.
